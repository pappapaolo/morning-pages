01 · The idea

What are Morning Pages?

Morning Pages are a daily stream-of-consciousness writing exercise popularized by Julia Cameron in The Artist’s Way. The traditional instruction is simple: write three longhand pages first thing in the morning about anything that crosses your mind.

They are not a diary entry you need to make meaningful. They are not a productivity report, a gratitude list, or a draft for anyone to read. The page can hold the petty thought, the impossible idea, the boring errand, the dream you almost forgot, and the sentence you repeat until another sentence appears.

The point is the uncensored motion of writing. By continuing past the obvious thoughts, you often reach the quieter ones underneath.

Morning Pages is a named creative practice, not a medical treatment. It may feel clarifying or calming, but this site does not make therapeutic or health claims.
02 · The method

How to write Morning Pages

  1. Arrive before the noise. If possible, write before messages, feeds, and work. If mornings are impossible, choose a repeatable quiet time.
  2. Write continuously. Put down whatever is in your head. Do not outline, research, fact-check, or make the sentences presentable.
  3. Aim for three pages. On paper, stop after three handwritten pages. Online, 750 words is a useful approximation—not a sacred conversion.
  4. Keep it private. Writing for an audience changes what you allow yourself to say. Treat the page as a private working space.
  5. Close it and continue your day. You do not need to analyze every entry. Let the practice accumulate before deciding what it means.

What if I have nothing to write?

Write “I do not know what to write” until it becomes untrue. Describe the room. List the thoughts competing for attention. Complain about the exercise. The resistance belongs on the page too.

Why 750 words?

Handwriting size, notebooks, margins, and spacing make an exact conversion impossible. Digital Morning Pages tools commonly use 750 words because it is a workable approximation of three handwritten pages and long enough to move beyond the first layer of thought.

03 · Paper or screen

Does typing count?

Longhand is the original format, and the slower physical pace can change how thoughts arrive. But typing is still a legitimate way to practice—especially when handwriting is painful, inaccessible, too slow, or simply the reason the habit never happens.

A good digital tool should preserve what matters about the exercise: privacy, continuity, low friction, and no pressure to perform. It should not turn private writing into content, score its emotional quality, or interrupt the page with prompts unless you ask.

Morning Pages uses a blank editor, a live word count, a gentle three-page progress bar, dated history, search, and optional sync. You can also export a backup. The tool is free.

04 · Keeping it going

A habit that can survive real mornings

Make the starting action tiny: open the same page, at roughly the same time, and type the first true sentence. Do not negotiate with the full 750 words before you have written word one.

  • Leave the editor bookmarked or make it your browser’s startup page.
  • Use the same device when you can, and sign in if you want history across devices.
  • Missed a day? Continue today. A streak is feedback, not a moral record.
  • If 750 words makes you avoid the practice entirely, begin smaller and grow into it.
  • Export a personal backup occasionally if the writing matters to you.
05 · Plain answers

Morning Pages questions

Can you do Morning Pages online or by typing?

Yes. The original practice emphasizes longhand, but typing can make it accessible and sustainable. Keep the essential conditions: write continuously, privately, and without editing yourself.

How many words are three Morning Pages?

There is no exact conversion because handwriting and paper vary. Many digital tools use 750 words as a practical approximation for three handwritten pages.

What should I write?

Anything present in your mind: plans, worries, complaints, dreams, fragments, repeated sentences, or “I do not know what to write.” It is stream of consciousness, not a polished journal entry.

Do they have to be written in the morning?

Morning is traditional because it catches the mind before the day becomes noisy. A consistent time you can keep is still more useful than repeatedly missing an ideal time.

Should I reread my Morning Pages?

You do not need to reread them immediately. Some practitioners leave early entries alone for weeks so the writing stays free from self-review. If you do revisit them, search for recurring ideas rather than grading the prose.

Is this an official Julia Cameron app?

No. This independent tool is inspired by the practice popularized in The Artist’s Way; it is not presented as an official Julia Cameron product.
